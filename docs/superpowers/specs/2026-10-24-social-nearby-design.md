# Conversation-first Nearby category

User asks for face-to-face games and questions rather than more mini arcades, and a clear split between Games (distance) and Nearby (together). Prior authorization covers design choices, inline execution, checking and publishing.

Move who, truths, taboo and wheel out of the remote catalogue into actual one-device versions in Nearby. Keep online engines for existing games/history/invitations. Exclude moved modes from new random remote invitations, home cards and favourites listing. Remove non-games cinema/capsule from the remote games list. Rename nav Games and Nearby, add explicit device descriptions and reciprocal navigation. Keep existing local activities, arcades and boards under Nearby; show conversations first and screen games under a collapsible lower section.

Add four new experiences: Wavelength (secret 1–10 target, spoken clue, partner guesses, alternate roles for six rounds); Seven Seconds (eight alternating rounds of naming three things, seven-second clock, human verdict); No Yes/No (four one-minute interviews, interviewer catches forbidden words or survivor wins); Conversation Cards (six questions with optional deeper follow-up, skip freely, no fake competitive score). At least 20 cards per new question bank; reuse existing who, taboo and wheel banks.

Shared pure immutable state machine, stable seeded decks, explicit secret handoff, timers that pause on blur/menu, local event replay saves, guarded actions and duplicate-move handling. No microphone, camera, login or second device required. The tablet supplies the game, people answer aloud. Scores are game points, manual outcomes are marked honestly. Restore on a secret stage requires a privacy shield before revealing it.

Approach: local social-game collection rather than adapting shared server sessions; this avoids requiring two accounts next to each other. User-facing choice is face-to-face/remote, not engine details. Existing sources and backend retained. Browser preview is still unavailable due to the existing policy block; do not bypass it.
